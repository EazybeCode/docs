Deal Analysis reviews each deal in your connected CRM together with the conversations linked to it, then records what it finds on the deal record.

It runs automatically on a recurring schedule. There is nothing to trigger, no per-deal setup, and no separate dashboard to open.

## Before You Start

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect your CRM">
    Deal Analysis works on deals in a connected CRM. Once the connection is active, deals are picked up automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach contacts to your deals">
    Conversations are matched to a deal through its associated contacts. A deal with no contacts attached is analysed from CRM data only.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Grant the sales-email-read scope (HubSpot only)">
    Email activity is read only if your HubSpot connection includes the **sales-email-read** scope. Recent versions of the integration request it automatically.

    To check, go to **Integration page → HubSpot settings → Scopes and permissions**. If the scope is not listed, reconnect your HubSpot account.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How It Works

Each analysis draws on two sources.

| Source | Data used |
|:-------|:----------|
| **CRM record** | Deal stage, amount, owner, close date, and pipeline, plus logged calls, meetings, tasks, emails, notes, and the associated contacts and companies |
| **Conversations** | Chats and email threads with the contacts on the deal, including message direction, timing, and how long each side took to reply |

The two sources are compared. Where the CRM record and the conversation differ, the difference is recorded rather than resolved.

Each run refreshes deals that have had new activity since they were last analysed. Every deal records the time of its most recent analysis.

<Note>
  Results are written to separate, dedicated fields. Existing deal fields such as stage, amount, and close date are never modified.
</Note>

## Where to Find It

Open any deal in your CRM. The analysis fields appear alongside the deal's existing fields on the record.

## What the Analysis Records

| Area | What it records |
|:-----|:----------------|
| **Response ownership** | Whether the deal is awaiting a reply from your team or from the customer |
| **Engagement trend** | Whether the conversation is speeding up or slowing down compared with its earlier pattern |
| **Inactivity** | Whether the customer has stopped replying |
| **Buying readiness** | Whether the customer is describing a requirement, evaluating your offering, or discussing price and terms |
| **Decision authority** | Whether your contact can approve the purchase or needs sign-off from someone else |
| **Stage accuracy** | Whether the conversation is ahead of, behind, or aligned with the recorded pipeline stage |
| **Close risks** | Competitor involvement, price resistance, silence after a quote, unmet requirements, slipping timelines, or a single engaged contact |
| **Objection** | The customer's most recent objection, in their own words |
| **Expected close timing** | The customer's stated timing, standardised into a fixed set of timeframes |
| **Discussed value** | The amount quoted or agreed in conversation |
| **Summary and next steps** | A plain-language summary of the deal and the recommended actions for it |
| **Supporting evidence** | The messages and activities behind each result, and the number of conversations read |

## Filtering and Reporting

Results are stored as standard deal fields, so they work in filters, saved views, dashboards, and reports like any other field. In your CRM's field list they appear together, under names beginning with **AI**.

Views teams commonly save:

- Deals awaiting a reply from your team
- Deals with a declining engagement trend
- Deals where the stage and the conversation disagree
- Deals expected to close in the current month

## Behaviour to Be Aware Of

- **An empty field means no signal was found.** Values are written only when there is supporting evidence. An empty field indicates the information was not present in the CRM or the conversations.
- **Inactivity is measured in days.** A deal is not marked inactive as soon as a reply is late. The customer must have left your last message unanswered for at least two days.
- **Values can change without new messages.** Inactivity and engagement trend are based on elapsed time and on comparison with earlier activity, so results can change during a period of silence.
- **Stated timings are standardised.** Phrases such as "in a few weeks" are converted into a fixed set of timeframes, calculated from the current date. Only closing commitments are counted, not scheduling for meetings or demos.
- **Manual edits are overwritten.** You can edit an analysis field directly, but the next scheduled run replaces the value. Use your own fields or a deal note to record your own assessment.

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Cause and resolution |
|:------|:---------------------|
| A deal shows no analysis | The deal has not been included in a run yet. New deals are picked up on the next scheduled run. A deal with no associated contacts and no logged activity has no data to analyse. |
| Email activity is missing | On HubSpot, the **sales-email-read** scope is not granted. Check **Integration page → HubSpot settings → Scopes and permissions** and reconnect the account if it is absent. |
| Values look out of date | Check the last analysed time on the deal. Deals are refreshed only when new activity has been recorded since the previous run. |
| Individual fields are empty | No supporting evidence was found for those fields. This is expected behaviour, not an error. |

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Will this change my existing CRM data?">
    No. Results are written to their own dedicated fields. Deal stage, amount, close date, and any other field your team maintains are never edited or overwritten. Where the analysis disagrees with the CRM record, it records the difference in its own field.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How often does it run?">
    On a recurring schedule. Each run refreshes deals that have had new activity since they were last analysed. Each deal shows when it was last analysed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How accurate is it?">
    Each result is linked to the message or activity it came from. If a value looks wrong, check the supporting evidence recorded on the deal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does it work with limited conversation history?">
    Yes, but with less data behind the results. Each deal records how many conversations were read, so you can judge how much a result is based on.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
