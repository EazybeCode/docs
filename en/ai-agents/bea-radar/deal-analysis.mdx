## What It Is

**BEA Radar — Deal Analysis** reads everything that has actually happened on a deal — your CRM record *and* the real conversations with the contacts on it — and writes a set of AI properties directly onto the HubSpot deal.

You don't leave HubSpot. You don't open another dashboard. You open a deal, and the AI properties are sitting there next to your own fields, telling you whether the deal is really moving, who owes the next reply, what the risk is, and when it is likely to close.

<Note>
  Deal Analysis works on **HubSpot deal records** and requires a connected HubSpot account. The values are written as standard HubSpot deal properties, so you can filter, sort, and report on them like any other field in your portal.
</Note>

## Where the Data Comes From

Every analysis is built from two sides.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="From HubSpot" icon="hubspot">
    The deal record and its properties (stage, amount, owner, close date, pipeline), all logged activities — calls, meetings, tasks — emails logged against the deal and its contacts, notes, and the associated contacts and companies.
  </Card>
  <Card title="From the conversation" icon="comments">
    Chat conversations (for example WhatsApp) with the contacts on the deal, email threads with those contacts, and the full back-and-forth — who sent what, when, and how long each side took to reply.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The AI then combines both. **This is where most of the value is:** the two sides often disagree. The CRM says one thing, the conversation says another — and catching that gap is the whole point of the feature.

## When It Runs

BEA Radar runs as a **scheduled batch on a fixed interval**. Each run picks up the deals that have had new activity since they were last analysed and refreshes their properties.

You don't trigger anything. You open a deal and the analysis is already there. The **AI Last Analysed At** property on every deal tells you exactly how fresh its values are.

### Risks it flags

**AI Risk Flag** is multi-select — a deal can carry more than one risk at once.

| Flag | Meaning |
|:-----|:--------|
| **Competitor** | The prospect mentions another company, or says someone else is offering a better deal |
| **Pricing** | The prospect says your price is too high, or pushes back on cost |
| **Went cold after pricing** | Price was shared and there has been no response since |
| **Requirement gap** | What they need is more than, or different from, what you're offering |
| **No response / ghosted** | The thread has gone silent — a loss risk in its own right |
| **Timeline slip** | Their stated timeline keeps moving further out |
| **Single threaded** | Only one contact is engaged; if they go quiet, the deal goes quiet |
| **None** | No risk signal found |

<Tip>
  This list is intentionally open-ended and will keep growing. The AI isn't restricted to a fixed menu — where a genuine risk pattern shows up in the conversation, it gets flagged.
</Tip>

## Key Behaviours to Know

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Nothing is guessed — blank means no signal">
    Every value written to a deal comes from something that actually happened in the CRM or the conversation. Where there is no real signal for a property, it is left **blank** rather than filled with an assumption.

    A blank field is information, not a gap. Filter for it and you get the list of deals where that signal genuinely doesn't exist yet.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Ghosted needs two days of silence">
    **AI Ghosted** turns Yes only when the last message came from your side, the reply is pending on the prospect's side, and **at least 2 days** have passed.

    The threshold is day-based on purpose. If a message goes out and the analysis runs 12 hours later, the prospect may simply not have got to it yet — so the flag would mean nothing. Two days makes it mean something real when it fires.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Closure timeline uses seven fixed values">
    Whatever the prospect says is normalised against today's date into one of seven phrases:

    `This week` · `Next week` · `2–3 weeks` · `This month` · `Next month` · `Next quarter` · `Beyond next quarter`

    Because the vocabulary is fixed, you can filter and report on it like a dropdown — *"show me everything closing this month"* works across your whole pipeline. Note this captures **closure** signals only: *"we'll sign next month"* counts, *"let's do the demo Friday"* does not.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Actual amount falls back to your CRM amount">
    If a deal amount is found in the conversations — a quote sent, a price agreed, a negotiated number — that value is used. If none is found, **AI Actual Deal Amount** falls back to the existing HubSpot deal amount.

    Your original HubSpot Amount field is left untouched. You keep both, and the difference between them is itself worth looking at — the CRM value is often a first estimate nobody went back and updated.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Stage Signal never overrides your CRM stage">
    **AI Stage Signal** compares the stage your conversation implies against the stage the deal actually sits in, and flags where they disagree. It is a flag only — your pipeline stays exactly where your team put it.

    *Ahead of stage* usually means the rep hasn't updated the CRM and your forecast is understated. *Behind stage* means the deal was moved forward optimistically and your forecast is overstated — usually the more expensive of the two.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Evidence and Verification

Every AI property is backed by evidence.

- **AI Evidence** — the message, quote, or activity that led to each conclusion. If the AI says *Buying Intent = High*, you can see the pricing question it read. Nothing is asserted without something to point at.
- **AI Chats Analysed** — how many conversations were fetched and analysed for that run. A confident-looking result built on two messages should be read differently from one built on sixty.
- **AI Last Analysed At** — the timestamp of the most recent run, so you always know how fresh the values are.

An internal integrity record of exactly what data went into each analysis is also kept, so a given result can be traced back to its inputs after the fact. It isn't something you need to read or act on.

## Reading the Signals Together

The properties are designed to be read as a set, not one at a time.

### A healthy deal

> Waiting On: **Prospect** · Trend: **Warming** · Ghosted: **No** · Intent: **High** · Decision Maker: **Yes** · Stage Signal: **Ahead of stage** · Risk: **None**

A commercial conversation with the right person, moving faster than your CRM knows. Update the stage and push to close.

### A quietly dying deal

> Waiting On: **Prospect** · Trend: **Cooling** · Ghosted: **No** · Intent: **Medium** · Decision Maker: **No** · Stage Signal: **Behind stage** · Risk: **Went cold after pricing**

Nothing here is on fire, and that's the point — this deal still looks fine in the pipeline. It isn't. Price landed badly, your contact can't approve it anyway, and engagement is falling. Intervene now, not at month-end.

### Needs you today

> Waiting On: **Us**

Whatever else is true, the prospect is waiting on you. Start here.

<Tip>
  Filter your pipeline for `AI Waiting On = Us` — that list is the fastest revenue action of your day. Then check `AI Engagement Trend = Cooling`, which fires while a deal still looks fine on paper, before the stage slips or the close date moves.
</Tip>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does this change my CRM data?">
    No. Every piece of analysis is written into its own dedicated property. Your existing fields — Deal Stage, Amount, Close Date, and everything else your team maintains — are never edited or overwritten. Where BEA Radar disagrees with your CRM, it says so in its own field and leaves yours alone.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How often does it run?">
    It runs as a scheduled batch on a fixed interval. Each run refreshes the deals that have had new activity since they were last analysed, so active deals stay current without you triggering anything. Check **AI Last Analysed At** on any deal to see when its values were last written.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why did a property change without new messages?">
    Because some properties measure time and silence, not just messages.

    **AI Ghosted** turns Yes the moment the 2-day threshold is crossed with no reply — nothing new has to arrive for that to happen.

    **AI Engagement Trend** is a comparison property: it measures how the conversation is running *now* against how it was running *before*. When messages stop, the comparison itself changes — the same deal that was *Steady* last week becomes *Cooling*, then *Gone dark*, purely because the pattern has shifted. The absence is the signal.

    **AI Risk Flag** follows from this too — once a deal is ghosted, *No response / ghosted* appears as a risk.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I filter and build views on these?">
    Yes. They're standard HubSpot deal properties, so you can filter on them, build your own list views, and create dashboards and reports using them — exactly like any other field in your portal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if the conversation data is thin?">
    BEA Radar will not guess. Every property reflects only what genuinely happened in the data available — if the evidence isn't there, the value isn't written. **AI Chats Analysed** tells you how many conversations the conclusion was built on, so you always know how much material is behind it.

    Where there's no signal at all the property stays blank, except **AI Decision Maker Involved**, which records this explicitly as *Unknown*.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is the AI ever wrong?">
    Accuracy is high, because no analysis stands on its own — every value is verified against an evidence record showing the exact message or activity it came from. Nothing is asserted that can't be traced back to something real in the conversation or the CRM.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I edit an AI property myself?">
    You can type over it in HubSpot, but the next scheduled run will overwrite it. If you want to record your own judgement permanently, use your own fields or a note on the deal.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
